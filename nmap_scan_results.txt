Question 1: What is the primary operational difference between running an Nmap scan with -sn versus running a scan with -sV?
Answer: The -sn flag disables port scanning and performs a pure host discovery sweep (ping sweep) at Layer 3 to check if a machine is online. The -sV flag performs active application banner-grabbing at Layer 4 and 7 by probing open ports to determine the exact service name and software version running behind them.

Question 2: Why is running a full port scan (-p-) considered critical during an active security assessment?
Answer: By default, Nmap only scans the top 1,000 most frequently used ports. Attackers and administrators frequently host sensitive or unauthorized services on non-standard ports (above port 1000) to avoid detection, making a full 1–65535 (-p-) scan vital to uncovering hidden exposure vectors.
